Observe
1. One letter is one write() system call, so one line costs 31 system calls (30 letters and the
   newline). Between two of them the process can be stopped (timer interrupt on one CPU, or the
   other process runs on another CPU) and the other process prints its letters in the middle of
   the line. Nothing keeps the 31 calls together.
2. The lines sem_down(3); before the loop with write() and sem_up(3); after it, in lines() of
   mix.c (both under if (uselock)). The loop between them is the critical section.
3. wakeup(s) wakes all the processes that sleep on the address of s. If two were waiting and
   sem_up made the value 1, both wake up, the first one that takes the lock decreases the value
   to 0, and the second finds 0 and must sleep again. With if it would go on and make the value
   -1, and two processes would be inside where only one may be.
4. sem_up needs the same spinlock to increase the value, so nobody could ever call it and the
   sleeper would wait for ever (on a CPU with interrupts off). Also sched() panics with
   "sched locks" if the process holds a spinlock other than its own p->lock. sleep_prepare(s)
   is called before the release, so a sem_up that comes before sleep() is not lost: sleep()
   sees the address was already woken and returns at once.
